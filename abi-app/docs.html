The two dimensions

The index treats anti-Black racism in a place and decade as something no single record measures directly. Each indicator below is a partial, noisy trace of it; the model combines them into two latent (unobserved) variables, estimated for every state and county in every decade from 1850 to 2010.

Hostility

Overt, interpersonal and organised hostility towards Black people: lynchings and race riots, intimidation and violence, the Klan and white-supremacist organisations, sundown towns, Confederate commemoration, votes for segregationist candidates, and the racial conflict recorded in the Black press. It is about what people did to Black residents and how openly they organised against them.

Treatment

Differential treatment by law and institutions: enslavement, Jim Crow and other state laws, the racial make-up of executions, imprisonment and police killings, redlining and school segregation. It is about how the legal and institutional order treated Black residents compared with white ones.

On both, higher values mean more anti-Black. The two are estimated jointly and are related (state-decade values correlate at about 0.4), but they can move apart: a state can repeal discriminatory laws while hostile violence persists, or the reverse.

Hostility indicators

Counts are modelled relative to the Black (or total) population. “Within decade” marks indicators whose coverage or life cycle changes over time, so they get a separate intercept in each decade: they rank places within a decade but do not drive change over time.

Treatment indicators

Shares of Black people among executions, prisoners or police killings are measured against the Black share of the population, so they capture over-representation rather than population size. Laws that protect Black residents count towards lower treatment values.

The three combined scores (Black press, punishment, police killings) each come from a separate one-dimensional model of indicators that measure nearly the same thing over the same years, so that each concept counts once however many records exist for it.

How the estimates are made

  1. State model. Each state’s value on each dimension follows a random walk from decade to decade, so a state’s past informs its present but can change. Every indicator is linked to one dimension with its own weight (loading) and baseline, using a likelihood that suits it: negative binomial for counts, beta-binomial for shares of Black people among a total, logistic for laws in force, normal (with known standard errors) for the combined and language-model scores. Lynchings and the Black share of executions fix each dimension’s scale.
  2. County model. With the state model’s indicator weights and scales held fixed, each county is estimated as a deviation from its state on both dimensions. The deviation follows an AR(1) process: evidence in one decade carries into neighbouring decades but fades back towards the state’s value where a county has no data. States are re-estimated alongside their counties, and the posterior is sampled with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (Stan), giving 95% intervals for every value.
  3. Boundaries. All records are placed in 2010 counties: events by place, and historical census counts through a crosswalk from historical to 2010 boundaries. A county appears from the decade it entered the data.

Values are on the model’s latent scale. Indicators measured the same way across the whole period carry information about change over time; those marked “within decade” only rank places within each decade. Comparisons between places in the same decade are therefore firmer than comparisons of levels decades apart.

Reading the map

  • Colours are seven quantile classes, each holding a seventh of all unit-decades. By default the classes are pooled over every decade (“All decades”), so colours compare across time; switch to “This decade” to rank units within the selected decade only.
  • Hatched counties have no estimate in that decade: they had not yet entered the data, which a county does once it exists and is settled densely enough. A few sparsely populated western and Alaskan counties therefore enter only after 1960.
  • Trajectories show the posterior mean with a 95% band. For a county, the dashed line is its state. Hollow points are county-decades with no county-level observations: their values rest on the state’s and on the county’s neighbouring decades, not on local evidence.
  • Percent Black comes from decennial census counts (including enslaved people before 1870), allocated to 2010 counties; state values sum their counties.
  • State values in the map are the county model’s, on the same scale as the counties.
